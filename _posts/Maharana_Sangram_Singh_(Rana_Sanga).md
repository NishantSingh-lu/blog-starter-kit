# A warrior is remembered not only for the battles he wins, but for the courage with which he fights and the ideals for which he stands.


Birth: Around 1482 CE
Reign: 1508–1528 CE
Dynasty: Sisodia Rajput Dynasty
Father: Rana Raimal of Mewar
Kingdom: Mewar
Capital: Chittorgarh
Known For: Uniting the Rajputs and challenging Babur.

# Early Life and Accession of Rana Sanga.
Many centuries ago, in the proud Rajput kingdom of Mewar, there stood the magnificent fortress of Chittorgarh Fort. The kingdom was ruled by a brave and respected king, Rana Raimal, a powerful ruler of the Sisodia dynasty known for his courage and strong administration.
Around the year 1482, a prince was born into this royal family. His name was Sangram Singh. At that time, no one could have imagined that this young child would one day become one of the greatest warriors in Indian history.
From a very young age, Sangram Singh grew up inside the mighty walls of Chittorgarh Fort. His childhood was not like that of ordinary children. As a Rajput prince, his daily life was filled with strict discipline and continuous training. Early in the morning, he practiced horse riding, learning how to control powerful war horses even at a young age. Throughout the day, he trained in sword fighting, archery, spear fighting, and military strategies. He was also taught the values of Rajput culture, where honour, bravery, loyalty, and sacrifice were considered more important than life itself. Along with physical training, he developed patience, observation skills, and a calm nature, which made him different from his brothers.
Sangram Singh was not the only son of Rana Raimal. The king had several sons, including Prithviraj Singh and Jagmal, who were also strong and ambitious. As the princes grew older, a serious question began to arise within the palace—who would become the next ruler of Mewar? This question slowly created tension among the brothers.
According to a popular traditional story, one day an astrologer visited the royal court. After carefully studying the stars and royal horoscopes, he made a bold prediction that Sangram Singh would one day rule Mewar. This prophecy deeply affected the royal family. While Sangram remained calm and silent, his brothers became jealous and suspicious. From that moment, the atmosphere in the palace changed, and rivalry began to grow stronger.
As time passed, this rivalry turned into open conflict. The brothers began fighting among themselves for power and influence. These struggles were extremely dangerous and violent. During these fierce conflicts, Sangram Singh suffered severe injuries. In one such attack, he lost one of his eyes. In another clash, his arm was badly injured, and his leg was also wounded, leaving him with a permanent limp. These injuries would have broken an ordinary person, but Sangram Singh showed extraordinary courage and mental strength.
Rana Sanga's body itself became a symbol of sacrifice and courage. He had lost one eye, one arm had been badly injured, and one of his legs was permanently damaged. It is often said that he carried dozens of battle wounds on his body. Every scar told the story of a warrior who had fought for honour and refused to surrender to fate.
To protect his life from further attacks, Sangram Singh had to leave the capital and live away from the royal palace for some time. During this difficult period, he travelled across different parts of Mewar and came into contact with many loyal Rajput chiefs and warriors. They were deeply impressed by his bravery, humility, and determination. Even after losing an eye and suffering multiple wounds, he continued to train himself and prepare for future challenges. Gradually, many nobles and chiefs began to believe that he was the rightful and most capable successor to the throne of Mewar.
Meanwhile, the kingdom itself was facing political instability. As Rana Raimal grew old and eventually passed away, the question of succession became more urgent. Different groups in the kingdom supported different princes, and there was a real danger that Mewar might fall into disorder and weakness.
At this critical moment, the nobles and warriors of Mewar carefully considered their choice. They realized that Sangram Singh, despite his injuries, possessed the qualities of a true ruler—courage, leadership, patience, and the ability to unite people. Therefore, they decided to support him.
With the strong backing of these nobles and chiefs, Sangram Singh challenged his rivals and successfully secured control over the kingdom. Finally, around 1508–1509, inside the grand halls of Chittorgarh Fort, he was crowned as the new ruler of Mewar.
From that day onward, Sangram Singh became known in history as Rana Sanga, the Maharana of Mewar.
The wounded prince who had once been forced to leave his home had now risen to the throne through courage, determination, and perseverance. His accession marked the beginning of a powerful era in which he would later unite many Rajput states and challenge some of the strongest rulers of his time.
The story of Rana Sanga’s early life and accession remains a powerful example of bravery, struggle, and determination. It teaches us that true greatness is not achieved easily, but through hardship, patience, and an unbreakable spirit. 

# Rise and Expansion of Power of Rana Sanga
After being crowned as the ruler of Mewar inside the mighty walls of Chittorgarh Fort, Sangram Singh, now known as Rana Sanga, did not allow himself to rest. The throne he had earned through struggle reminded him not of victory, but of responsibility. He knew that the kingdom he ruled had been weakened by internal conflicts, and if he did not act wisely, it could easily fall apart.
In the early days of his rule, instead of rushing into wars, he focused on restoring order within Mewar. The nobles were divided, and loyalties were uncertain. Rana Sanga began calling them to his court, listening to their concerns, settling disputes, and slowly rebuilding trust. He rewarded those who were loyal and dealt firmly with those who created disorder. His decisions were calm but strong, and gradually, peace returned to the kingdom. The people began to feel secure, and confidence in their ruler started to grow.
At the same time, he turned his attention to the army. He believed that a ruler must be a warrior first. He personally inspected the training of soldiers, ensuring that they were disciplined and prepared for battle. The cavalry, which formed the backbone of Rajput warfare, was strengthened with well-trained horsemen and powerful war horses. Weapons were carefully maintained, and soldiers practiced regularly with swords, spears, and bows. It is said that sometimes Rana Sanga himself would join the training sessions, showing the soldiers how to fight, reminding them that their king was not above them but among them. This created a deep sense of loyalty, and the army became fearless and united.
The great fortress of Chittorgarh was also strengthened under his rule. Its walls were repaired, its defenses improved, and supplies of food and weapons were stored carefully. Other forts across Mewar were also secured so that the kingdom would remain safe even in times of attack. Slowly but steadily, Rana Sanga transformed Mewar into a strong and stable power.
However, he knew that strength within the kingdom was not enough. Beyond Mewar, the Rajputs were divided into many small states, often fighting among themselves. This disunity had made them weak for years. Rana Sanga understood that if the Rajputs continued to remain divided, they would never be able to stand against powerful enemies.
Instead of trying to conquer them by force, he chose a wiser path. He began reaching out to other Rajput rulers, inviting them to form alliances. He treated them with respect and honour, not as enemies but as equals. Many rulers were impressed by his courage, humility, and sense of justice. They saw in him a leader who truly cared for Rajput pride and unity.
Gradually, one by one, many Rajput chiefs and clans, including the Rathores and Chauhans, began to support him. A sense of unity, which had long been missing, started to grow again. For the first time in many years, Rajputs stood together under a common leadership. Rana Sanga had successfully created a powerful confederacy, where strength came not just from numbers, but from shared purpose.
With a strong kingdom behind him and united Rajput support, Rana Sanga began expanding his influence. He challenged powerful rulers of his time and emerged victorious in several battles. His success against Ibrahim Lodi of the Delhi Sultanate increased his reputation across North India. He also defeated Mahmud Khilji II and fought successfully against Muzaffar Shah II, expanding his control into important regions.
Each victory strengthened his position. His territory expanded, his army grew larger, and his name spread far and wide. Some rulers willingly accepted his leadership, while others feared opposing him. His court became a place where warriors gathered, alliances were formed, and strategies were planned.
At the height of his power, Rana Sanga was no longer just the ruler of Mewar. He had become the most powerful king in North India. His influence extended across large regions, and he was seen as the strongest hope for Rajput unity and strength. People began to believe that he had the power to challenge even the greatest forces and restore the glory of the Rajputs.
Thus, through patience, strength, unity, and continuous effort, Rana Sanga rose from a wounded prince to a mighty ruler whose power and influence shaped the history of his time.

# Battle of Khatoli (1517)
The year was 1517, and the growing power of Rana Sanga of Mewar had begun to worry the Sultan of Delhi, Ibrahim Lodi. News of the Rajput ruler’s strength, his disciplined army, and his rising influence had reached Delhi, and Ibrahim Lodi realized that if this power was not checked, it could become a serious threat to his rule. At the same time, Rana Sanga also understood that a clash with the Delhi Sultanate was inevitable. The tension between the two rulers slowly turned into open confrontation, and both sides prepared for war.
Near the fields of Khatoli, the two armies finally faced each other. The army of Ibrahim Lodi was large and well-equipped, while Rana Sanga’s forces were smaller in number but filled with courage and determination. As the sun rose, the battlefield came alive with the sound of war drums and the cries of soldiers preparing for combat. Dust rose into the air as horses moved forward and warriors tightened their grip on their weapons.
When the battle began, it was fierce and intense. Rajput warriors charged forward fearlessly, striking with swords and spears, while the Sultan’s forces responded with equal strength. In the middle of this chaos, Rana Sanga stood at the front, leading his army personally. He did not stay behind like many rulers; instead, he fought among his soldiers, encouraging them with his presence.
As the battle continued, a powerful attack from the enemy struck Rana Sanga. He was badly wounded and suffered serious injuries. For a moment, it seemed as if he might fall from his horse. Seeing this, some of his soldiers rushed towards him and urged him to leave the battlefield to save his life. But Rana Sanga refused. Despite his injuries, he chose to continue fighting, believing that a king should never abandon his men in the middle of war.
His courage had a powerful effect on his army. When the soldiers saw their king fighting even after being severely wounded, their determination grew stronger. They fought with renewed energy and refused to step back. Slowly, the balance of the battle began to change. The forces of Ibrahim Lodi, despite their size, started to lose confidence and began to retreat under the continuous attack of the Rajput warriors.
After hours of intense fighting, the army of the Delhi Sultan was defeated. The battlefield of Khatoli fell silent, leaving behind the signs of a hard and bloody struggle. Rana Sanga had won a great victory. Though he was seriously injured, his success spread across North India, and his reputation as a fearless and powerful ruler grew stronger than ever before.
This battle was not just a victory over a strong enemy; it was a moment that proved Rana Sanga’s extraordinary courage and leadership. Even in pain and danger, he stood firm, inspiring his soldiers and defeating a mighty force. The Battle of Khatoli became a symbol of bravery, showing that true strength lies not in numbers, but in determination and spirit.

# Battle of Dholpur (1518)
The victory at Khatoli had not only strengthened the power of Rana Sanga of Mewar, but it had also shaken the confidence of the Sultan of Delhi, Ibrahim Lodi. The defeat was a blow to his pride, and he was determined to restore his authority. He could not accept that a Rajput ruler had challenged and defeated him so boldly. Therefore, instead of withdrawing, Ibrahim Lodi prepared once again for war.
Rana Sanga, on the other hand, was aware that this conflict was far from over. Even after suffering serious injuries in the previous battle, his spirit remained unbroken. He knew that the next encounter would be even more important, as it would decide whether his earlier victory was temporary or a sign of his rising dominance.
In the year 1518, the two forces once again moved towards each other, this time near Dholpur, a strategically important region. The atmosphere was tense, as both sides understood the importance of this battle. For Ibrahim Lodi, it was a chance to recover his lost prestige, and for Rana Sanga, it was an opportunity to prove his strength once again.
As the armies gathered on the battlefield, the contrast between them was clear. The Sultan’s army was large and well-equipped, confident in its numbers and resources. Rana Sanga’s army, though smaller, was filled with experienced warriors who had already tasted victory. They carried with them the confidence gained from Khatoli and a deep trust in their leader.
When the battle began, it was fierce and determined from both sides. The soldiers of Delhi fought with the aim of revenge, while the Rajputs fought with courage and unity. Rana Sanga once again chose to lead from the front. Despite his previous injuries, he appeared on the battlefield, mounted on his horse, inspiring his soldiers simply by his presence.
The fighting continued with great intensity. Dust and noise filled the battlefield as swords clashed and arrows flew through the air. At several moments, the outcome seemed uncertain, as both armies pushed against each other with full strength. However, Rana Sanga’s leadership and the discipline of his army slowly began to make a difference.
Seeing their ruler standing firm despite his wounds, the Rajput soldiers fought with even greater determination. Their unity and courage gave them an advantage over the larger but less coordinated forces of Ibrahim Lodi. Gradually, the Delhi army began to lose its formation, and signs of weakness started to appear.
As the battle progressed, the forces of Ibrahim Lodi were pushed back. What began as a determined attempt to regain honour slowly turned into a struggle for survival. Eventually, the Delhi army was defeated once again. Ibrahim Lodi was forced to retreat, accepting another setback at the hands of Rana Sanga.
The battlefield of Dholpur fell silent, marking another important victory for Rana Sanga. This second defeat of the Delhi Sultan was far more significant than the first. It proved that the victory at Khatoli was not a matter of chance, but a clear indication of Rana Sanga’s growing power.
After this battle, his reputation spread even further across North India. Many rulers began to see him as a dominant force, while others hesitated to challenge him. The people and warriors of Mewar felt immense pride, as their ruler had defeated one of the most powerful sultans of the time not once, but twice.
Thus, the Battle of Dholpur became another important step in the rise of Rana Sanga. It strengthened his position, increased his influence, and confirmed his status as one of the most powerful and fearless rulers of his time. 

# Battle of Gagron (1519)
After his repeated victories over the Sultan of Delhi, the power and reputation of Rana Sanga of Mewar had spread far and wide. His name was now spoken with respect and even fear across North India. But his ambition was not limited to defending his kingdom—he aimed to expand his influence and establish strong control over neighboring regions.
To the south-east of Mewar lay the rich and strategically important region of Malwa, ruled by Mahmud Khilji II. The politics of Malwa were unstable, and its ruler was facing internal weaknesses. Seeing this as an opportunity, Rana Sanga decided to move against Malwa, knowing that controlling this region would greatly strengthen his power.
In the year 1519, the armies of Rana Sanga and Mahmud Khilji II met near the strong fort of Gagron. The fort stood as an important defensive position, surrounded by rivers and natural barriers, making it difficult to attack. The Sultan of Malwa was confident that his position would protect him, but he underestimated the determination and strategy of Rana Sanga.
As the two armies prepared for battle, the atmosphere was filled with tension. The soldiers of Malwa stood ready to defend their land, while the Rajput warriors, already confident from previous victories, prepared themselves for another test of strength. Rana Sanga, as always, took his position at the front, ready to lead his army into battle.
The fighting began with great intensity. The forces of Malwa tried to use the natural defenses of Gagron to their advantage, while Rana Sanga’s army advanced with discipline and courage. The battle was not easy, as the terrain made movement difficult, and the enemy fought fiercely to protect their stronghold.
In the midst of the conflict, Rana Sanga showed his skill not only as a warrior but also as a strategist. He carefully directed his forces, finding ways to overcome the defensive advantage of the enemy. His leadership kept his army united and focused, even in the difficult conditions of the battlefield.
As the battle continued, the Rajput forces slowly gained the upper hand. Their determination and coordination began to break the defenses of the Malwa army. The soldiers of Mahmud Khilji II started losing confidence as they saw their lines weakening under continuous attack.
Finally, the forces of Rana Sanga broke through, and the army of Malwa was defeated. Mahmud Khilji II himself was captured during the battle. It was a moment of complete victory for Rana Sanga, who had successfully overcome a strong and well-defended opponent.
However, what followed made this victory even more remarkable. Instead of treating the defeated Sultan with cruelty, Rana Sanga chose to show generosity and honour. He later released Mahmud Khilji II, demonstrating the Rajput code of respect and dignity even towards a defeated enemy.
This act greatly increased his reputation. He was no longer seen only as a powerful warrior, but also as a noble and honourable ruler. His influence over Malwa grew, and his control over the region strengthened his overall position.
The Battle of Gagron thus became a turning point in his rise. It not only expanded his territory but also enhanced his prestige across India. His victories were now not just about strength, but also about character and leadership.
With each battle, Rana Sanga moved closer to becoming the most powerful ruler of his time, and the victory at Gagron stood as a shining example of his courage, strategy, and honour.

# Malwa Campaign
After his decisive victory at Gagron, Rana Sanga of Mewar did not stop his advance. The defeat and capture of Mahmud Khilji II had shaken the foundations of the Malwa Sultanate. Although Rana Sanga had shown generosity by releasing the Sultan, the political condition of Malwa remained weak and unstable.
Rana Sanga understood that this was the right moment to secure long-term control over the region. Malwa was not just another kingdom—it was rich in resources, strategically located, and important for trade routes. If it remained unstable, it could become a threat again in the future. But if brought under control, it would greatly strengthen his power.
Thus began his continued campaigns in Malwa.
Instead of a single battle, this was a series of movements, decisions, and actions aimed at establishing dominance. Rana Sanga moved carefully, sending his forces into key areas of Malwa. Some local rulers and chiefs, already weakened by internal conflicts, chose to accept his authority rather than resist. Others attempted to oppose him but were defeated by his disciplined and experienced army.
As his forces advanced, forts and important towns began to fall under his influence. The Rajput army moved steadily, securing positions and strengthening control over the region. Rana Sanga ensured that his commanders maintained discipline and order, so that the newly gained territories could be managed properly.
During this time, Rana Sanga showed not only his strength as a warrior but also his skill as a ruler. He did not simply conquer land—he worked to stabilize it. Local chiefs were allowed to retain their positions if they accepted his authority, which helped in maintaining peace. This policy reduced resistance and made his control more effective.
Gradually, large parts of Malwa came under his influence. His authority in the region became strong enough that he could control its politics and use its resources to strengthen his own kingdom. Trade routes passing through Malwa now benefited Mewar, increasing wealth and stability.
These campaigns further increased his reputation. He was no longer just a victorious warrior—he was now seen as a ruler capable of managing and expanding a large territory. His power extended beyond Mewar into Malwa and neighboring regions, creating a wide sphere of influence.
With Malwa under his control and Gujarat campaigns adding to his strength, Rana Sanga reached the peak of his power. His kingdom was strong, his allies were united, and his enemies had been defeated or weakened. He stood as the most powerful ruler in North and Western India.
But this rise also brought new challenges. As his influence expanded, he began to attract the attention of a new and ambitious ruler who had recently entered India—Babur. The stage was now set for a much greater conflict, one that would test the full strength of Rana Sanga.

# Gujarat Campaigns (1519–1520)
The power and influence of Rana Sanga of Mewar reached new heights. His success against Malwa had not only expanded his territory but also strengthened his confidence and reputation. Across North and Western India, rulers began to watch his movements carefully, knowing that he had become a force that could no longer be ignored.
To the west of Mewar lay the wealthy and powerful kingdom of Gujarat, ruled by Muzaffar Shah II. Gujarat was known for its riches, strong administration, and well-equipped army. However, like many other regions of that time, it was also facing internal challenges and political tensions. Rana Sanga saw in this situation an opportunity to further expand his influence and secure his position as the most powerful ruler in the region.
Around 1519–1520, Rana Sanga began his campaigns towards Gujarat. Unlike a single battle, these campaigns consisted of several military actions and encounters. His army moved steadily into the regions connected to Gujarat, facing resistance from the Sultan’s forces at different points.
As always, Rana Sanga led with confidence and determination. His army, strengthened by previous victories, fought with discipline and unity. The Rajput warriors advanced with courage, while the forces of Gujarat tried to defend their territories. The clashes were intense, and the outcome was not always immediate, but Rana Sanga’s experience and leadership gave him an advantage.
In several encounters, the forces of Gujarat were defeated, and Rana Sanga was able to assert his dominance over important areas. His campaigns disrupted the power of the Gujarat Sultan and weakened his control over certain regions. Step by step, Rana Sanga expanded his influence further westward.
These victories were important not just for territorial expansion, but also for strategic reasons. By gaining control over regions connected to Gujarat, Rana Sanga strengthened trade routes and increased the resources available to his kingdom. His power was no longer limited to Mewar and Malwa—it now extended towards western India as well.
With each success, his reputation grew stronger. Rulers who had once doubted him now recognized his strength, while others chose to form alliances rather than oppose him. His army became larger and more confident, and his leadership was admired across regions.
The Gujarat campaigns showed that Rana Sanga was not just a warrior who could win individual battles, but a ruler capable of conducting long and complex military campaigns. His ability to maintain pressure over time and achieve success in multiple engagements proved his skill as both a strategist and a leader.
By the end of these campaigns, Rana Sanga had firmly established himself as the dominant power in a large part of North and Western India. His influence stretched across Mewar, Malwa, and regions connected to Gujarat, making him the most powerful ruler of his time.
These campaigns marked another important step in his rise, bringing him closer to the peak of his power. But as his strength continued to grow, so did the challenges before him. Soon, he would face a new and powerful opponent who had arrived from beyond India’s borders, and this would lead to one of the most decisive moments in his life.
 
# Arrival of BABUR 
At the beginning of the 16th century, the political condition of North India was weak and divided. The once-powerful Delhi Sultanate was no longer as strong as it had been before. Internal conflicts, disloyal nobles, and poor leadership had weakened its foundation. The ruler at that time, Ibrahim Lodi, faced opposition not only from outside enemies but also from within his own kingdom. Many nobles were unhappy with him, and some even began to look for another leader who could replace him.
Far away in Central Asia, there lived a young and ambitious ruler named Babur. He was a descendant of great conquerors and had inherited both courage and determination. However, his early life was full of struggles. He lost and regained territories many times and faced constant challenges from rival rulers. Although he managed to establish control over Kabul, he was not satisfied. He dreamed of building a larger and more stable empire, one that would bring him lasting power and glory.
Babur’s eyes soon turned toward India. He had heard of its immense wealth, fertile lands, and political weakness. To him, India was not just a land to raid—it was a land to conquer and rule. But Babur was not careless. He did not rush into a full invasion immediately. Instead, he began by making small, careful expeditions into the northwestern regions of India. These early invasions allowed him to study the land, understand the strengths and weaknesses of Indian armies, and plan his future strategy.
At the same time, some nobles of the Delhi Sultanate, unhappy with Ibrahim Lodi, secretly invited Babur to invade India and overthrow the Sultan. This gave Babur both an opportunity and a reason to act. He now had support from within India, which made his plan stronger.
By 1525, Babur was fully prepared. He gathered his army, which was not very large but was highly disciplined and well-organized. What made his army different was its use of gunpowder weapons, including cannons and matchlock guns. These weapons were still new in Indian warfare, and most Indian armies were not familiar with them.
As Babur marched towards India, his army crossed rivers, mountains, and difficult terrain. His journey was not easy, but his determination remained strong. He used careful planning, maintained strict discipline among his soldiers, and kept his army united with a clear goal—to defeat Ibrahim Lodi and establish his rule.
Finally, in 1526, Babur reached the plains of Panipat, where he faced the army of Ibrahim Lodi. Ibrahim Lodi’s army was much larger, filled with thousands of soldiers and war elephants. But Babur had something different—strategy and technology.
Before the battle, Babur arranged his army in a unique formation, using carts and ropes to create defensive lines and placing cannons strategically. When the battle began, the sound of cannons echoed across the battlefield, creating fear and confusion in the enemy ranks. The war elephants, which were a major strength of Ibrahim Lodi’s army, became uncontrollable due to the noise and chaos.
The battle was intense, but Babur’s tactics slowly turned the situation in his favor. His disciplined soldiers and effective use of artillery broke the enemy lines. In the end, Ibrahim Lodi was defeated and killed on the battlefield.
With this victory, Babur captured Delhi and Agra, marking the beginning of a new rule in India. This was not just another invasion—it was the foundation of the Mughal Empire, which would shape the future of India for centuries.
News of Babur’s victory spread quickly across the land. Powerful rulers began to take notice of this new force. Among them was Rana Sanga, who had himself become the most powerful Rajput ruler of his time. He understood that Babur was not like earlier invaders who came for loot and left. Babur intended to stay, rule, and expand his empire.
Thus, the arrival of Babur marked a turning point in Indian history. It brought a new kind of warfare, a new kind of leadership, and a new challenge for the rulers of India. It set the stage for a great conflict between the rising Mughal power and the established Rajput strength led by Rana Sanga—a conflict that would soon unfold in the battles of Bayana and Khanwa.


# Preparation of Rana Sanga Against Babur
When the news reached Rana Sanga that Babur had defeated Ibrahim Lodi and taken control of Delhi, a deep silence fell over the court of Mewar. This was not just another victory of one king over another—it was the arrival of a new kind of power. Babur had not come like earlier invaders who looted and left; he had come with a clear intention to stay, rule, and expand.
Rana Sanga understood the seriousness of this moment better than anyone else. Sitting in the great halls of Chittorgarh, surrounded by his nobles and warriors, he listened as messengers described Babur’s army—its discipline, its strange weapons that produced fire and thunder, and its powerful strategies. For a few moments, the court remained quiet, as everyone realized that the coming war would be unlike any they had fought before.
One evening, as the sun set behind the walls of Chittorgarh Fort, Rana Sanga called his closest commanders for a private council. Maps were spread across the floor, and the situation was carefully discussed. Some nobles advised attacking quickly before Babur could strengthen his position, while others suggested waiting and observing. Rana Sanga listened patiently, his face calm but serious. Finally, he spoke with firm determination that this would not be a hurried war—it would be a carefully prepared struggle for the future of their land.
From that moment, preparations began on a massive scale.
Messengers rode across Rajputana, carrying Rana Sanga’s call for unity. They traveled to distant forts and courts, inviting Rajput rulers to join him. His message was not one of command, but of shared honour and duty. He reminded them that this was not just his battle—it was a battle for all Rajputs. Slowly, the response began to come. Chiefs and kings, who had once been divided by rivalry, started to gather under his leadership. One by one, banners arrived in Mewar, each representing a clan ready to fight.
At the same time, Afghan nobles who had lost their position after Babur’s victory also began to approach Rana Sanga. They were experienced warriors, familiar with the strength of the Mughal army, and they offered their support. Rana Sanga accepted them carefully, knowing that unity would give him the strength needed to face such a powerful enemy.
Soon, the lands around Mewar turned into a vast war camp. Soldiers trained from dawn to dusk. The sound of swords clashing, horses galloping, and war drums beating filled the air. Weapons were sharpened again and again until they shone like mirrors. Armour was repaired and strengthened. Supplies of food, water, and equipment were gathered in large quantities, as everyone knew that a long and difficult campaign was ahead.
Rana Sanga did not remain distant from these preparations. Despite the injuries he had suffered in earlier battles—his lost eye, his wounded arm, and his limp—he moved among his soldiers. He watched their training, spoke to them, and encouraged them. His very presence inspired confidence. The soldiers saw in him not just a king, but a warrior who had suffered and fought like them.
One day, while inspecting the army, a young warrior hesitated and asked him, “Maharana, Babur’s army is said to have weapons that roar like thunder… how can we face such power?” Rana Sanga looked at him with calm strength and replied, “Weapons do not win wars—courage does. And courage lives in the heart, not in iron.”
These words spread quickly through the camp, filling the soldiers with determination.
Meanwhile, Rana Sanga also began planning his strategy. He knew that Babur’s strength lay in his new weapons and disciplined formations. To face this, he decided to rely on the traditional strength of the Rajputs—fast cavalry, powerful charges, and fearless combat. He planned to use speed and unity to break the enemy’s lines before they could fully use their advantage.
Days turned into weeks, and weeks into months. The army grew larger, stronger, and more united. Confidence spread among the warriors, and a belief took hold that they were fighting not just for victory, but for honour and independence.
As the time of battle approached, Rana Sanga stood ready. His preparations were complete, his army united, and his purpose clear. He knew that the coming conflict would decide the future of North India.
Thus, through careful planning, unity, discipline, and unbreakable determination, Rana Sanga prepared himself for one of the greatest challenges of his life—a challenge that would soon lead to the battles of Bayana and Khanwa, where history itself would be shaped.

# Battle of Bayana 
After months of careful preparation, when Rana Sanga finally began his march against Babur, the land itself seemed to hold its breath. This was no ordinary conflict. It was a clash between two powerful forces—one representing the united strength of the Rajputs, and the other bringing new weapons and strategies from Central Asia.
As Rana Sanga’s massive army advanced, banners of different Rajput clans waved proudly in the wind. Warriors from various regions marched together, many of whom had once been rivals but were now united under a single purpose. The sound of war drums echoed across the plains, and the movement of thousands of soldiers created an atmosphere filled with anticipation and determination.
Their first major target was Bayana, a strong and strategically important region. It lay between the advancing Rajput forces and the Mughal-held territories. Babur clearly understood its importance and had stationed his forces there to block Rana Sanga’s advance. The Mughal commanders knew that if Bayana fell, it would open the path for Rana Sanga towards Agra and Delhi.
As the Rajput army approached Bayana, the Mughal forces prepared themselves for defense. They positioned their troops carefully, relying on discipline and formation. However, unlike the later battle of Khanwa, their preparations here were not yet fully developed. They had not completely adapted to the powerful and fast Rajput style of warfare.
One early morning, as the mist slowly lifted from the battlefield, both armies stood facing each other. The silence before the battle was heavy, broken only by the distant sounds of horses and armour. Then, with a sudden roar, the Rajput army charged forward.
Rana Sanga led from the front, as he always did. Despite his injuries, he rode with confidence, inspiring his warriors. The Rajputs attacked with great speed and force. Their cavalry moved like a storm, striking the Mughal lines before they could properly respond. Swords clashed, spears struck, and the battlefield quickly turned into a scene of chaos and courage.
The Mughal forces tried to hold their ground, but the intensity of the Rajput attack was overwhelming. Their formations began to weaken under the repeated charges. The speed and coordination of Rana Sanga’s army created confusion among the Mughal soldiers, who struggled to reorganize themselves.
At several points during the battle, the fighting became extremely close. Warriors fought face to face, showing immense bravery. The Rajputs, driven by honour and unity, refused to step back. Their determination slowly began to turn the tide of the battle.
Seeing the strength of Rana Sanga’s assault, the Mughal forces started to lose confidence. Their defensive lines broke, and they were pushed back from their positions around Bayana. What had begun as a defensive stand slowly turned into a retreat.
This moment marked a significant success for Rana Sanga. The Rajput army had proven that Babur’s forces were not invincible. The victory filled the Rajput camp with confidence and hope. Soldiers celebrated, believing that if they could defeat the Mughals here, they could do so again in the final battle.
However, while the Rajputs celebrated, Babur observed carefully. He understood that this encounter had exposed a weakness in his army. He realized that the traditional Rajput charge was powerful and difficult to stop. Instead of ignoring this, he learned from it.
In the days that followed, Babur began to prepare more seriously. He reorganized his army, strengthened his defensive formations, and planned to use his artillery more effectively. He knew that the next battle would be decisive, and he could not afford to make the same mistakes again.
Thus, the Battle of Bayana was not just a victory for Rana Sanga—it was also a turning point in the conflict. It gave confidence to the Rajputs, but at the same time, it forced Babur to adapt and prepare for a much greater challenge.
The stage was now set for the final confrontation. Both sides had tested each other’s strength. Both had learned important lessons, and the stage was now set for the Battle of Khanwa, where the fate of North India would be decided. After the Battle of Bayana, the conflict between Rana Sanga and Babur became more intense, leading to the decisive Battle of Khanwa in 1527.

# Battle of khanwa 
After the encounter at Bayana, both Rana Sanga and Babur understood that the next battle would decide everything. There could be no second chances. One would emerge as the supreme power of North India, and the other would fall.
As winter faded and the early months of 1527 arrived, both armies began moving toward a vast plain near a village called Khanwa, not far from Agra. The land was wide and open, perfect for a massive confrontation. Slowly, the area filled with thousands of soldiers, horses, weapons, and war drums. The air itself seemed heavy with expectation, as if the earth knew that history was about to change.
On one side stood Rana Sanga, at the head of a grand Rajput confederacy. His army was not just from Mewar—it included warriors from many Rajput clans, along with Afghan allies who opposed Babur. Their banners fluttered together in the wind, representing unity, courage, and honour. Despite the wounds he had suffered in earlier battles—his lost eye, his injured arm, and his limp—Rana Sanga stood tall, a symbol of resilience and bravery. To his soldiers, he was not just a king but a living example of sacrifice and strength.
On the other side stood Babur, calm but fully alert. He had carefully studied the Rajput style of warfare after Bayana. He knew that their strength lay in their fast and powerful cavalry charges, which could break even strong armies. This time, he was determined not to allow that to happen.
Babur spent days preparing his battlefield. He ordered carts to be tied together with strong ropes, forming a defensive wall in front of his army. Behind these barriers, he placed his cannons and matchlock soldiers. His plan was simple but effective—stop the Rajput charge, weaken them with artillery, and then strike back with discipline.
On the night before the battle, both camps were restless. In the Rajput camp, warriors prayed, sharpened their swords, and spoke of honour and sacrifice. Many knew that they might not return, but none were willing to step back. In the Mughal camp, Babur addressed his soldiers, reminding them of the importance of this battle. He encouraged them to remain disciplined and trust in their strategy.
As the sun rose over Khanwa, the battlefield came alive.
The silence broke with the thunder of war drums and the cries of soldiers. Rana Sanga raised his sword, and with a powerful command, the Rajput army charged forward like a storm. Their cavalry moved with incredible speed, aiming to break through the Mughal lines just as they had done in previous battles.
For a moment, the ground trembled under the force of their charge.
But then, something different happened.
From behind the Mughal defenses, the cannons roared. Loud explosions filled the air, and thick smoke spread across the battlefield. The sound was terrifying, especially for those who had never faced such weapons before. The Rajput horses became restless, and the soldiers struggled to maintain their formation.
Despite this, the Rajputs did not retreat. They pushed forward bravely, reaching the Mughal lines and engaging in fierce hand-to-hand combat. Swords clashed, spears struck, and the battlefield turned into a scene of chaos and courage. Rana Sanga himself fought in the front, encouraging his men and leading by example.
The battle continued for hours. The Rajputs fought with unmatched bravery, but Babur’s strategy began to show its strength. His army remained organized, and the repeated use of artillery weakened the Rajput attacks. Every time the Rajputs tried to break through, they were met with resistance and gunfire.
In the midst of this intense fighting, tragedy struck. Rana Sanga was seriously wounded, and blood flowed from his injuries. Yet he refused to leave the battlefield. Holding his sword tightly, he continued to encourage his warriors and fought with unshaken courage. Only when he became too weak to continue did his loyal chiefs surround him and carry him away by force, fearing that they might lose their Maharana forever.
As news of his injury spread, the morale of the Rajput army began to fall. Their leader, their source of strength, was no longer visible on the battlefield. Confusion and uncertainty spread among the ranks.
Babur saw this moment and acted decisively. He ordered his forces to advance and press the attack. The disciplined Mughal army moved forward, maintaining formation and continuing their assault. Slowly, the Rajput resistance weakened.
By the end of the day, the battlefield of Khanwa belonged to Babur.
The Rajput army was defeated, and the dream of stopping the Mughal advance was shattered. It was a hard-fought battle, filled with courage and sacrifice, but the combination of Babur’s strategy, discipline, and new technology proved decisive.
After the battle, Rana Sanga, though alive, was deeply affected. Even in defeat, he did not lose his spirit. He wished to recover, gather his forces again, and continue the struggle against Babur. But fate had other plans. Before he could fight another battle, he died around 1528.
The Battle of Khanwa became one of the most important turning points in Indian history. It firmly established the power of Babur in North India and marked the decline of Rajput political dominance, despite their unmatched bravery.  Khanwa was not just a battle—it was a decisive moment of history.

# Death of Rana Sangram Singh (RANA SANGA)
As the long and brutal day of battle at Khanwa came to an end, the battlefield slowly fell into silence. The air, once filled with the roar of cannons and the clash of swords, now carried only the sounds of wounded soldiers and the cries of the fallen. The dust of war settled over the ground, covering the signs of one of the fiercest battles ever fought. Victory had gone to Babur, but the cost had been heavy on both sides.
Amidst this scene of destruction lay Rana Sanga, gravely wounded. His body bore deep injuries from the battle—his armour was broken, and blood had soaked into his clothes. Yet even in this condition, his face did not show fear. It showed pain, but also strength, as if his spirit still refused to accept defeat.
His loyal warriors rushed to him, forming a protective circle. They knew that if he remained on the battlefield, he could be captured or killed. Carefully, with great effort, they lifted him and began to carry him away from Khanwa. As they moved, many soldiers looked at their fallen leader with tears in their eyes. For them, he was not just a king, but the symbol of their pride and unity.
As Rana Sanga was taken away, he slowly regained consciousness. His first thoughts were not about his wounds, but about the battle. In a weak voice, he asked about the condition of his army. When he was told that the Rajput forces had been defeated, he remained silent for a long moment. It was not the silence of surrender, but of deep thought.
Even in defeat, his will remained unbroken.
In the days that followed, he was taken to a safe place where his wounds could be treated. Physicians tried their best to heal him, and his loyal chiefs stayed constantly by his side. His body was weak, but his mind remained sharp and determined. He spoke of the future, not of defeat. He spoke of gathering strength again, of calling back his allies, and of preparing for another war against Babur.
This determination, however, created fear among his nobles.
They had seen the terrible power of Babur’s army—the cannons, the disciplined formations, and the devastating effect of gunpowder. They knew that another direct confrontation could destroy what remained of their strength. While they respected and loved Rana Sanga, they also feared that his unbreakable spirit might lead them into another disaster.
In the quiet corners of the court, discussions began among the nobles. Some believed that continuing the war was necessary to protect honour. Others believed that survival was more important than another risky battle. This division created tension among those who had once stood united.
According to traditional accounts, this fear and uncertainty led to a tragic decision. Some of his closest nobles, unable to oppose him openly and afraid of another war, are said to have secretly decided to stop him in the only way they thought possible. It is believed that they administered poison to him, hoping to prevent further bloodshed.
As days passed, Rana Sanga’s condition worsened. Whether due to his wounds, exhaustion, or the alleged poisoning, his strength began to fade. The once powerful warrior, who had faced countless battles and survived terrible injuries, now lay weak but still dignified.
In his final moments, it is said that he remained calm. There was no fear in him—only the quiet strength of a man who had lived as a true warrior. Around him stood his loyal followers, many of them unable to hold back their tears. They knew they were witnessing the end of an era.
Around the year 1528, Rana Sanga passed away.
With his death, the great leader who had united the Rajputs and challenged some of the most powerful rulers of his time was gone. His dream of defeating Babur and restoring Rajput dominance remained unfulfilled, but his legacy did not fade.
The news of his death spread quickly across the land. In Mewar, people mourned the loss of their brave ruler. Even his enemies respected him, for they knew they had faced a warrior of extraordinary courage.
Though defeated at Khanwa, Rana Sanga was remembered not for his loss, but for his bravery, leadership, and unbreakable will. He had fought against overwhelming odds, united divided clans, and stood as a symbol of resistance.
Thus ended the life of Rana Sangram Singh Sisodia, better known as Rana Sanga—a warrior whose body bore the scars of countless battles but whose spirit remained unbroken. Though he could not stop the rise of the Mughal Empire, he united the Rajputs, challenged mighty rulers, and became a symbol of courage, sacrifice, and resistance. His name continued to inspire future generations, especially his grandson, Maharana Pratap. Even today, Rana Sanga is remembered not for his defeat at Khanwa, but for his bravery, leadership, and unwavering determination, making him one of the greatest warrior-kings in Indian history.
